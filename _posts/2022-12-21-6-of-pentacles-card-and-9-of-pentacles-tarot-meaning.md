However, if you have been practising single card readings for a while a 2 card spread will be a great introduction into reading multiple cards.

## What does 6 Of Pentacles and 9 Of Pentacles mean together?

The 6 Of Pentacles tarot card and The 9 Of Pentacles tarot card together suggest that you have the opportunity to gain financial luxury and gain self-sufficiency through generosity and helping or receiving help. This could mean that someone within your circle or even further afield is offering assistance, or you could be in a position to provide the same. The combination of these two cards speaks of the power of giving and of being rewarded with the privilege of enjoying your own pleasures and luxuries.

## Common 2-card spreads

Whilst it’s great to know who these two cards interact with each other, it’s important to note that the meaning will depend on the context of your spread

The most common 2 card spreads I recommend to newer readers are:

- The 2 card cross (otherwise known as “The Fool’s Journey”)
- The 2 card “Yes or No”

### “The Fool’s Journey”

Setup: The two card cross is where you place two cards face up, one placed vertically and with one laying horizontally over

Interpretation: The 2 cards represent a “situation” and a “challenge”. The first card will describe the situation you are in with the second horizontal card being the “challenge” crossing you (literally crossing you in this case!)

Reading: Using the overviews in this guide is a great way to start. First, look at the meanings of the 2 cards, and see how they might relate either as your “situation” or “challenge”. Once you have that you can look at the general overview for how the two cards interact with each other.

The benefit of this spread is that it can give clarity when you’re feeling uncertain or anxious. Once you have clarity of thought, and a strong idea of what your challenge is you can find solutions to help your situation

### The 2 card “Yes or No”

Setup: The 2 card “Yes or No” is where you place two cards face up side by side, whilst asking your deck a “yes or no” question.

Interpretation: Each card in a tarot deck has a “yes” or a “no” meaning. Look at the meanings of each card, and you will have your answer.

The benefit of this is you get a stronger yes or no compared to a single card draw. You may find you often get a mix of “yes or no”, and this is fine. It simply means there is doubt, or things are not yet clear.

## What does 6 Of Pentacles mean?

On the other hand, it can be interpreted as a reminder to take care of our bodies and minds in constructive, sustainable ways. It encourages us to think about our lifestyle choices, and how they impact our physical, mental, and emotional well-being. In terms of health care, this card may indicate a person who is willing to seek out professional help, or to offer financial or emotional support to someone who needs it.

Finances: The 6 of Pentacles Tarot card indicates that any financial problems can be solved with careful planning and a balanced approach. The card speaks to managing our income and expenses in a sustainable way, so that there is enough money to cover our needs and leave room for some luxury too. It encourages us to think about our goals, and to plan a financially responsible path to reach them. In terms of investments, this card may suggest that a wise and prudent approach is needed, where risk taking is kept to a minimum. Finally, The 6 of Pentacles Tarot card can represent charitable giving, where a person is generous with their resources, in order to benefit others.

Relationships: The 6 of Pentacles Tarot card speaks to the importance of mutual support and generosity in relationships, as well as the need for creating a balance between give and take. It encourages us to think about ways to nurture a relationship and ensure that both partners’ needs are met. This card can also indicate a person who is willing to share their resources, in order to help those they care about. Finally, it serves as a reminder that relationships are built on mutual respect and understanding; while it might be tempting to keep score of who has done what, a healthier approach is to accept that both partners have equal needs and rights.
