However, if you have been practising single card readings for a while a 2 card spread will be a great introduction into reading multiple cards.

The 3 Of Swords card and Queen Of Cups have unique qualities and will interact with each other in different ways depending on what kind of reading you are doing.

## What does 3 Of Swords and Queen Of Cups mean together?

The 3 of Swords and the Queen of Cups tarot cards together represent a difficult period of severe loss and heartbreak combined with a healing and supportive presence in your life. The 3 of Swords speaks to the pain and sorrow of loss, the experience of divorce or the depression of a breakup. At the same time, the Queen of Cups offers a source of comfort and understanding, inviting you to reach out for the compassion and care you need to heal. With her presence, you can move through this difficult phase in your life and come out the other side with renewed strength and appreciation for the good that still exists in your life.

## Common 2-card spreads

Whilst it’s great to know who these two cards interact with each other, it’s important to note that the meaning will depend on the context of your spread

The most common 2 card spreads I recommend to newer readers are:

- The 2 card cross (otherwise known as “The Fool’s Journey”)
- The 2 card “Yes or No”

### “The Fool’s Journey”

Setup: The two card cross is where you place two cards face up, one placed vertically and with one laying horizontally over

Interpretation: The 2 cards represent a “situation” and a “challenge”. The first card will describe the situation you are in with the second horizontal card being the “challenge” crossing you (literally crossing you in this case!)

Reading: Using the overviews in this guide is a great way to start. First, look at the meanings of the 2 cards, and see how they might relate either as your “situation” or “challenge”. Once you have that you can look at the general overview for how the two cards interact with each other.

The benefit of this spread is that it can give clarity when you’re feeling uncertain or anxious. Once you have clarity of thought, and a strong idea of what your challenge is you can find solutions to help your situation

### The 2 card “Yes or No”

Setup: The 2 card “Yes or No” is where you place two cards face up side by side, whilst asking your deck a “yes or no” question.

Interpretation: Each card in a tarot deck has a “yes” or a “no” meaning. Look at the meanings of each card, and you will have your answer.

The benefit of this is you get a stronger yes or no compared to a single card draw. You may find you often get a mix of “yes or no”, and this is fine. It simply means there is doubt, or things are not yet clear.

## What does 3 Of Swords mean?

The 3 of Swords Tarot card is a warning about the difficult times ahead and serves as a reminder to prepare for hardships that may come our way. The card often represents heartbreak, divorce, loss, depression and surgery. In terms of health, the card is a warning that things might not go as planned and it is important to be aware of the potential for physical or emotional pain. Financially, the card can signal financial loss as a result of a difficult situation, such as divorce or job loss. In relationships, the card could indicate a difficult experience such as betrayal, breakups, or loss of intimacy.

In terms of health, the 3 of Swords Tarot card serves as a reminder to be proactive in preventing physical and emotional pain. Taking care of the body and mind is key in order to cope with the negative feelings associated with this card. It is important to recognize any symptoms of depression, anxiety or other health issues, since they can become harder to manage if they are not addressed in a timely manner.

In terms of finances, the 3 of Swords Tarot card warns us to prepare for the potential of financial loss, as this could be a difficult time. It is important to be mindful of the expenses that may arise during this time and to look for opportunities to save money.

Finally, in terms of relationships, the 3 of Swords Tarot card can point to a difficult situation. This card can also appear when there is a need to confront issues in the relationship, as well as when one person needs to take responsibility for their actions. It is important to take the time to assess the situation and be honest with oneself and one’s partner in order to find a path forward.

## What does Queen Of Cups mean?

The Queen of Cups Tarot card is associated with many positive qualities. It suggests that a person is nurturing, supportive, and has a healing energy. This card can represent a compassionate and loving person who is capable of offering emotional support and healing, both to those they know and to strangers. For health, it can mean that a person has a deep understanding of their own body and what it needs, and is in tune with the body and its needs. This card can also represent a person who is capable of understanding and managing their emotional health, and is able to provide emotional support to others who might be struggling.

For finances, the Queen of Cups Tarot card can symbolize a person who is financially stable and secure. This card can indicate a person who is able to manage their finances responsibly and wisely, understanding their own financial needs, and those of others. It can also mean a person who is generous, and is willing and able to support those in need.

For relationships, the Queen of Cups Tarot card indicates a person who is deeply understanding, supportive, and caring. This card suggests that a person is capable of being emotionally available and listening to the needs of their partner, as well as offering emotional support and healing. They are also likely to be generous both in terms of finances and emotional energy. This card can also indicate a person who is willing to take risks and look to the future in relationships, while still staying grounded in the present. The Queen of Cups Tarot card is a reminder to stay open and trusting in relationships, while also being mindful of your own personal boundaries.
