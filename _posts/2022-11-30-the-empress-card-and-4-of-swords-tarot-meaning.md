## Common 2-card spreads

Whilst it’s great to know who these two cards interact with each other, it’s important to note that the meaning will depend on the context of your spread

The most common 2 card spreads I recommend to newer readers are:

- The 2 card cross (otherwise known as “The Fool’s Journey”)
- The 2 card “Yes or No”

### “The Fool’s Journey”

Setup: The two card cross is where you place two cards face up, one placed vertically and with one laying horizontally over

Interpretation: The 2 cards represent a “situation” and a “challenge”. The first card will describe the situation you are in with the second horizontal card being the “challenge” crossing you (literally crossing you in this case!)

Reading: Using the overviews in this guide is a great way to start. First, look at the meanings of the 2 cards, and see how they might relate either as your “situation” or “challenge”. Once you have that you can look at the general overview for how the two cards interact with each other.

The benefit of this spread is that it can give clarity when you’re feeling uncertain or anxious. Once you have clarity of thought, and a strong idea of what your challenge is you can find solutions to help your situation

### The 2 card “Yes or No”

Setup: The 2 card “Yes or No” is where you place two cards face up side by side, whilst asking your deck a “yes or no” question.

Interpretation: Each card in a tarot deck has a “yes” or a “no” meaning. Look at the meanings of each card, and you will have your answer.

The benefit of this is you get a stronger yes or no compared to a single card draw. You may find you often get a mix of “yes or no”, and this is fine. It simply means there is doubt, or things are not yet clear.

It symbolizes a pause from the hustle and bustle of everyday life – a welcome recess from the external and internal conflict that can take its toll. In order to truly rebuild and restore balance, the mind, body, and spirit must all be nurtured and refreshed.

In terms of health, the Four of Swords Tarot card can suggest a call to relax and recuperate. This can represent taking a break from the stresses of life, allowing the body and mind to recalibrate. It is a reminder to maintain strong self-care practices such as yoga, meditation, or sufficient sleep. Taking the time to slow down and restore mental and emotional energy can help build a stronger immune system and improve overall physical health.

When it comes to finances, the Four of Swords Tarot card can indicate a need to step back and reassess one’s financial situation. The pause this card represents allows one to evaluate their resources and ensure they are being used wisely. It is also a sign to stay patient and not rush into any financial commitment. This card is a strong indicator to consider if a situation is right for you, and if it’s worth the potential risks.

In terms of relationships, the Four of Swords Tarot card encourages a period of solitude and renewal. It suggests taking a moment to reflect on one’s needs, and learning to be okay with being alone. It doesn’t mean to push others away, but rather to focus on yourself. This can be a time for soul searching and discovering what truly makes you happy. It is important to respect and understand the need for self-care and personal growth, as it can benefit both you and your relationships.

The Four of Swords Tarot card is a reminder that rest and renewal are essential in maintaining balance in one’s life. It can be a much needed call to slow down, assess the situation, and ensure the mind, body, and spirit are all taken care of. It is a card that helps us recognize the importance of the pause and take the time to seek out ways to restore our inner peace and balance.
