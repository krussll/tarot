A key aspect of reading your own tarot is interpreting cards. For a beginner interpreting a single card can be fine, as there are plenty of resources to help you out, however interpreting two or more can be very tricky.

However, if you have been practising single card readings for a while a 2 card spread will be a great introduction into reading multiple cards.

As you’ve found this page, you’re probably wondering how to interpret the The Sun card and The World card together in particular.

The The Sun card and The World have unique qualities and will interact with each other in different ways depending on what kind of reading you are doing.

## What does The Sun and The World mean together?

The Sun tarot card and The World tarot card mean that you can expect a joyous outcome and the successful completion of a lesson or goal. It is a sign of the end of a journey or cycle, and with it comes the gifts of material happiness and joy. You may be presented with the opportunity to travel and see the world, or enter a new cycle of learning and accomplishment. All in all, these two cards offer a positive outlook for your future endeavors and the promise of a rewarding experience.

The meaning may differ depending on what you are asking. Here are some common questions and their possible meanings

## Common 2-card spreads

Whilst it’s great to know who these two cards interact with each other, it’s important to note that the meaning will depend on the context of your spread

The most common 2 card spreads I recommend to newer readers are:

- The 2 card cross (otherwise known as “The Fool’s Journey”)
- The 2 card “Yes or No”

### “The Fool’s Journey”

Setup: The two card cross is where you place two cards face up, one placed vertically and with one laying horizontally over

Interpretation: The 2 cards represent a “situation” and a “challenge”. The first card will describe the situation you are in with the second horizontal card being the “challenge” crossing you (literally crossing you in this case!)

Reading: Using the overviews in this guide is a great way to start. First, look at the meanings of the 2 cards, and see how they might relate either as your “situation” or “challenge”. Once you have that you can look at the general overview for how the two cards interact with each other.

The benefit of this spread is that it can give clarity when you’re feeling uncertain or anxious. Once you have clarity of thought, and a strong idea of what your challenge is you can find solutions to help your situation

### The 2 card “Yes or No”

Setup: The 2 card “Yes or No” is where you place two cards face up side by side, whilst asking your deck a “yes or no” question.

Interpretation: Each card in a tarot deck has a “yes” or a “no” meaning. Look at the meanings of each card, and you will have your answer.

The benefit of this is you get a stronger yes or no compared to a single card draw. You may find you often get a mix of “yes or no”, and this is fine. It simply means there is doubt, or things are not yet clear.

## Yes or No meaning

**Both The Sun and The World mean “Yes” when being asked a question. There is no doubt here, if you draw The Sun and The World the answer to your query is “YES”.**

## What does The Sun mean?

Health: The Sun Tarot card offers a strong symbol of vitality, which can have a connection to physical health. It could mean having energy for day-to-day tasks, improved health due to better eating and exercise habits, or being free from illness and disease. It could also mean a higher sense of well-being and contentment. It could even suggest taking part in activities that bring joy, as well as improving one’s health on a physical, mental and emotional level.

Finances: The Sun Tarot card symbolizes material success, which can be a great comfort for those who are struggling financially. It could mean a steady job with a good salary, the ability to pay bills, support a family, and have leisure money to relax and have fun. The card could also be a sign of a long-term dream coming true, such as finally investing in an expensive item or service, like a car or vacation, or finding success with a side business or new career.

Relationships: The Sun Tarot card is a symbol of joy and celebration, which can do wonders for relationships, both intimate and platonic. As such, it could mean finding love, solving a long-standing argument with a friend, or mending a broken bond with a family member. It could also suggest taking part in activities together that bring joy and fulfillment, such as going out for dinner, having a picnic in the park, or going on a spiritual retreat. The Sun Tarot card could also mean showing compassion and appreciation for the people in one’s life, as well as being surrounded by true friends and unconditional love.

## What does The World mean?

The World Tarot card often reflects the idea of completion and new beginnings, which can be quite meaningful when it comes to personal health, finances and relationships.

When it comes to health, The World Tarot card can signify the successful completion of a health goal such as overcoming a physical ailment or exercising regularly. It can also indicate the chance to begin anew, such as when transitioning to a healthier lifestyle or diet. This card encourages changes for the better, and can be a reminder to continue the good work you’ve been doing in order to live a healthier life.

For finances, The World Tarot card is often associated with increased wealth and success in business. It can represent the conclusion of a financial goal such as repaying all debts, or investing in a new venture. This card can also symbolize opportunities to make more money, perhaps through a bonus or raise at work. On a deeper level, it reinforces the idea that money matters are best managed with a sense of balance and stability.

When it comes to relationships, The World Tarot card can signify a successful completion of a relationship goal or challenge. It may indicate the end of the honeymoon period of a relationship, or signify the transition from friendship to something more serious. On a larger scale, it can represent a much-needed break or the completion of a difficult period. This card is a symbol of being emotionally grounded and open to new beginnings.
