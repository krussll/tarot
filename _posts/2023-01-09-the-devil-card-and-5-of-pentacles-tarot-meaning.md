A key aspect of reading your own tarot is interpreting cards. For a beginner interpreting a single card can be fine, as there are plenty of resources to help you out, however interpreting two or more can be very tricky.

However, if you have been practising single card readings for a while a 2 card spread will be a great introduction into reading multiple cards.

As you’ve found this page, you’re probably wondering how to interpret the The Devil card and 5 Of Pentacles card together in particular.

The The Devil card and 5 Of Pentacles have unique qualities and will interact with each other in different ways depending on what kind of reading you are doing.

## What does The Devil and 5 Of Pentacles mean together?

When The Devil tarot card and The 5 Of Pentacles tarot card come together, it suggests a period of struggle, either emotionally or financially. It could signal a need to watch out for any addiction problems or obsessions, as these could cause a strain on finances or lead to extreme worry. The card combination could indicate a fear of financial loss or poverty, as well as difficulties with relationships, as envy and lust could create discord in a partnership. With this card combination, it is important to be aware of any unhealthy behaviors or habits that are preventing progress, as this could worsen any isolation that is already being experienced.

The combination of these two cards suggests that you are in serious danger of becoming too attached to someone, to the point of addiction. The influence of this card can also lead to financial loss, as you may find yourself spending more and more money on the person you are addicted to.

On the other hand, the 5 of Pentacles can point to the isolation and worry that comes with such a relationship. The person you are addicted to may not be able to provide you with the emotional support that you need, leaving you feeling alone and anxious. This card can also point to times of difficult financial periods, when money may be tight and resources may be scarce.

Overall, the combination of these two cards suggests that you should be aware of your feelings and take time to reassess your relationships. If you feel that you are getting too attached to someone, it may be time to reevaluate the situation and seek help if necessary. It is important to remember that, while relationships can bring immense joy, it is equally important to ensure that those connections are healthy and not destructive to your life.

## Common 2-card spreads

Whilst it’s great to know who these two cards interact with each other, it’s important to note that the meaning will depend on the context of your spread

The most common 2 card spreads I recommend to newer readers are:

- The 2 card cross (otherwise known as “The Fool’s Journey”)
- The 2 card “Yes or No”

### “The Fool’s Journey”

Setup: The two card cross is where you place two cards face up, one placed vertically and with one laying horizontally over

Interpretation: The 2 cards represent a “situation” and a “challenge”. The first card will describe the situation you are in with the second horizontal card being the “challenge” crossing you (literally crossing you in this case!)

Reading: Using the overviews in this guide is a great way to start. First, look at the meanings of the 2 cards, and see how they might relate either as your “situation” or “challenge”. Once you have that you can look at the general overview for how the two cards interact with each other.

The benefit of this spread is that it can give clarity when you’re feeling uncertain or anxious. Once you have clarity of thought, and a strong idea of what your challenge is you can find solutions to help your situation

### The 2 card “Yes or No”

Setup: The 2 card “Yes or No” is where you place two cards face up side by side, whilst asking your deck a “yes or no” question.

Interpretation: Each card in a tarot deck has a “yes” or a “no” meaning. Look at the meanings of each card, and you will have your answer.

The benefit of this is you get a stronger yes or no compared to a single card draw. You may find you often get a mix of “yes or no”, and this is fine. It simply means there is doubt, or things are not yet clear.

## What does The Devil mean?

The Devil Tarot card is a symbol of the dark aspects of human nature. It can indicate a person’s tendency to become easily tempted by their vices and desires. On one hand, the card represents the addictive qualities of certain behaviours, such as the compulsive need for material possessions and money. On the other hand, it can signifies the dark side of relationships, such as envy and possessiveness.

When it comes to health, The Devil Tarot card can be an indication of a person’s unhealthy obsession with things such as their physical appearance, food, drugs and alcohol. This unhealthy reliance on such external things can lead to unhealthy habits, negative thought patterns and damaging behaviours. The Devil Tarot card is a representation of the need to be always in control or the tendency to give in to one’s temptations, even when it could be damaging to one’s health in the long run.

The Devil Tarot card can also represent issues related to finances. When it appears in a reading, it can signify the tendency to become too caught up in the pursuit of money, leading to the neglect of important needs and responsibilities. It can also be indicative of the trap of consumerism, as it can signal a person’s need for a quick and easy fix that often does not last. This temptation can lead to overspending and debts.

Finally, The Devil Tarot card can be a warning sign of unhealthy relationships, as it can represent possessive, jealous and controlling behaviour. This card can signal a lover’s tendency to be overly dependent on their partner, or to become overly consumed by their relationship. It can also point to a partner’s tendency to manipulate or take advantage of their partner for their own benefit. In any case, the card encourages the need to balance out one’s excessive desires and vices, so that a healthy and prosperous relationship can prevail.

## What does 5 Of Pentacles mean?

The 5 of Pentacles Tarot card is associated with feelings of ill-health, poverty and worry. It suggests that an individual is feeling disconnected and insignificant in their relationships, both with themselves and with the people around them. This Tarot card means that they may be worried about their financial situation and feel that they are not achieving the level of success they desire. This worry can be further amplified by feelings of isolation, as their lack of finances means they may be unable to pursue the activities they once enjoyed.

With regards to health, the 5 of Pentacles Tarot card can signify physical and mental exhaustion, as well as general feelings of ill-health. It may represent a person’s unwillingness or inability to take care of their physical or mental health, or an individual feeling overwhelmed by their current circumstances. It can represent a person struggling with the limitations of their current situation.

Financially, the 5 of Pentacles Tarot card indicates a period of difficulty and insecurity. This may manifest as a lack of income, public assistance or family support. It suggests that the individual may not have the resources to meet their basic needs. This can lead to feelings of worry and anxiety as they worry about how they will meet their financial obligations.

In relationships, the 5 of Pentacles Tarot card can signify a disconnect between the individual and their family, friends and partners. It can represent feelings of being excluded or of not belonging. This can be further exacerbated by the individual’s inability to provide for their family. It can also suggest a lack of mutual support or understanding from their peers as they try to cope with their current situation.

In conclusion, The 5 of Pentacles Tarot card can represent a time of ill-health, financial insecurity, worry and isolation. It can be a difficult period of adversity, however with the right support, individuals can use this time to learn more about themselves and to build resilience in order to come out of this period stronger and more prepared for the future.
