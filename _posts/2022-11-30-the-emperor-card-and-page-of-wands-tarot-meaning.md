However, if you have been practising single card readings for a while a 2 card spread will be a great introduction into reading multiple cards.

The Page of Wands tarot card also promotes creativity, suggesting that it is the perfect time for you to take up a new project or initiative that can be beneficial to both you and those that you lead. Together, the Emperor tarot card and the Page of Wands tarot card are a reminder that you have the power to make a difference in the world by taking action and being a leader.

## Common 2-card spreads

Whilst it’s great to know who these two cards interact with each other, it’s important to note that the meaning will depend on the context of your spread

The most common 2 card spreads I recommend to newer readers are:

- The 2 card cross (otherwise known as “The Fool’s Journey”)
- The 2 card “Yes or No”

### “The Fool’s Journey”

Setup: The two card cross is where you place two cards face up, one placed vertically and with one laying horizontally over

Interpretation: The 2 cards represent a “situation” and a “challenge”. The first card will describe the situation you are in with the second horizontal card being the “challenge” crossing you (literally crossing you in this case!)

Reading: Using the overviews in this guide is a great way to start. First, look at the meanings of the 2 cards, and see how they might relate either as your “situation” or “challenge”. Once you have that you can look at the general overview for how the two cards interact with each other.

The benefit of this spread is that it can give clarity when you’re feeling uncertain or anxious. Once you have clarity of thought, and a strong idea of what your challenge is you can find solutions to help your situation

### The 2 card “Yes or No”

Setup: The 2 card “Yes or No” is where you place two cards face up side by side, whilst asking your deck a “yes or no” question.

Interpretation: Each card in a tarot deck has a “yes” or a “no” meaning. Look at the meanings of each card, and you will have your answer.

The benefit of this is you get a stronger yes or no compared to a single card draw. You may find you often get a mix of “yes or no”, and this is fine. It simply means there is doubt, or things are not yet clear.

## What does The Emperor mean?

The Emperor Tarot card represents power, structure, and leadership. In terms of health and well-being, this card encourages us to take ownership of our own health. We should practice self-care and respect our bodies, as the Emperor demands respect and attention. The Emperor is also a protector, so we should use our own strength to protect our physical and mental health. We should also be vigilant of our environment and the way it can affect our health, and make use of our capacity to lead and create a healthier world.

In terms of finances and money, the Emperor encourages us to take a responsible, structured approach to our finances. We should think strategically and plan ahead, budgeting carefully and investing wisely. We should strive for a sense of financial stability and security, so that our future is assured and we can make wise decisions about where to invest our assets. The Emperor also urges us to recognise our own capabilities and use them to our advantage—we should not rely on others for financial assistance unless absolutely necessary.

Finally, the Emperor Tarot card offers guidance in terms of relationships. We should strive for strong, balanced relationships with those around us; relationships where both sides have equal power and respect. We should practice active listening and ensure open communication to ensure our relationships are as healthy and fulfilling as possible. We should also be mindful of our own needs and that of others, and strive to achieve a workable balance. The Emperor guides us to be strong and to take an authoritative role in our relationships, while still allowing room for compromise and negotiation.
