A key aspect of reading your own tarot is interpreting cards. For a beginner interpreting a single card can be fine, as there are plenty of resources to help you out, however interpreting two or more can be very tricky.

However, if you have been practising single card readings for a while a 2 card spread will be a great introduction into reading multiple cards.

As you’ve found this page, you’re probably wondering how to interpret the Justice card and Ace Of Wands card together in particular.

The Justice card and Ace Of Wands have unique qualities and will interact with each other in different ways depending on what kind of reading you are doing.

## What does Justice and Ace Of Wands mean together?

This project may require a great deal of work, but will be worth it in the end. As it progresses, you can expect that the truth will come out, allowing for win-win solutions. You will be inspired and have the urge to create this new project, as the forces of Justice and creation come together to form a powerful force.

## Common 2-card spreads

Whilst it’s great to know who these two cards interact with each other, it’s important to note that the meaning will depend on the context of your spread

The most common 2 card spreads I recommend to newer readers are:

- The 2 card cross (otherwise known as “The Fool’s Journey”)
- The 2 card “Yes or No”

### “The Fool’s Journey”

Setup: The two card cross is where you place two cards face up, one placed vertically and with one laying horizontally over

Interpretation: The 2 cards represent a “situation” and a “challenge”. The first card will describe the situation you are in with the second horizontal card being the “challenge” crossing you (literally crossing you in this case!)

Reading: Using the overviews in this guide is a great way to start. First, look at the meanings of the 2 cards, and see how they might relate either as your “situation” or “challenge”. Once you have that you can look at the general overview for how the two cards interact with each other.

The benefit of this spread is that it can give clarity when you’re feeling uncertain or anxious. Once you have clarity of thought, and a strong idea of what your challenge is you can find solutions to help your situation

### The 2 card “Yes or No”

Setup: The 2 card “Yes or No” is where you place two cards face up side by side, whilst asking your deck a “yes or no” question.

Interpretation: Each card in a tarot deck has a “yes” or a “no” meaning. Look at the meanings of each card, and you will have your answer.

The benefit of this is you get a stronger yes or no compared to a single card draw. You may find you often get a mix of “yes or no”, and this is fine. It simply means there is doubt, or things are not yet clear.

## Yes or No meaning

**Things are a bit tricky for this combination, as Justice and Ace Of Wands mean different things. The cards are unsure about your question, or the answer to the question has not yet been decided.**

The “Yes” and “No” meanings can differ from reader to reader. The meanings here are based on what I believe are the generally accepted definitions.

## What does Justice mean?

The Justice Tarot card has a strong association with fairness and balance. As a result, it has a lot to say about health, finances, and relationships.

In terms of health, the Justice Tarot card encourages us to strive for balance in all areas of our life, including mental and physical health. It calls for us to be mindful of our choices and be conscious of our well-being. This card is a reminder to nourish our bodies and take the time to practice self-care.

The Justice Tarot card is a guiding force in terms of finances. It reminds us to live within our means and to strive for balance between income and spending. The card encourages us to take a proactive approach to our finances and to be responsible with our resources. This can mean budgeting, saving, investments or other responsible financial practices.

Finally, the Justice Tarot card speaks to relationships. This card calls for fairness and equal exchange between people in all areas of life. It encourages healthy communication, mutual respect, and trust. It also reminds us to be honest with our intentions and to practice clear boundaries in our relationships. The card is a reminder to be mindful of the needs of others and to put effort into making relationships meaningful.

The Justice Tarot card stands for balance and equity in all areas of our life. The card encourages us to practice self-care and financial responsibility, as well as fairness and respect in our relationships. When we practice this sort of balance, we can experience positive outcomes in all three areas of our life.

## What does Ace Of Wands mean?

The Ace of Wands Tarot card is a great sign regarding health, finances and relationships. It is a reminder to look at the bigger picture of what we want out of life and to take action to make it happen. This card encourages you to take risks and to be ambitious. For one’s health, it suggests that you take ownership of your well-being and to look for ways to improve it.This could mean taking time for exercise and proper nutrition, seeking out medical advice if needed, and looking into better ways to organize the time we devote to our health.

For finances, this card encourages us to be resourceful and creative in our approach to money management. It also can indicate a potential increase in income, either through a new job opportunity or an existing work situation. Taking small steps towards financial security, such as creating an emergency fund and budgeting wisely, are advised.

In terms of relationships, this card suggests that you take the time to communicate effectively and take risks when it comes to connecting with another person. An open mind and heart is necessary for a healthy relationship and the Ace of Wands encourages us to lead with our intuition and focus on the long-term potential of the situation. It also implies that new relationships may arise unexpectedly, so be open to unexpected changes, as well as the possibility of new connections.
