## What does The Tower and King Of Swords mean together?

The Tower tarot card and The King Of Swords tarot card together represent an unpleasant combination of force and change. This could mean that you may find yourself in an upheaval caused by a powerful leader’s strategic actions. This could lead to unwanted changes, ruin, disgrace, and violence in your life, creating a difficult and troubling situation. It could also mean that it is time to make hard decisions, and some powerful force or leader must be dealt with, if you are going to come out of this with any peace or dignity.

## Common 2-card spreads

Whilst it’s great to know who these two cards interact with each other, it’s important to note that the meaning will depend on the context of your spread

The most common 2 card spreads I recommend to newer readers are:

- The 2 card cross (otherwise known as “The Fool’s Journey”)
- The 2 card “Yes or No”

### “The Fool’s Journey”

Setup: The two card cross is where you place two cards face up, one placed vertically and with one laying horizontally over

Interpretation: The 2 cards represent a “situation” and a “challenge”. The first card will describe the situation you are in with the second horizontal card being the “challenge” crossing you (literally crossing you in this case!)

Reading: Using the overviews in this guide is a great way to start. First, look at the meanings of the 2 cards, and see how they might relate either as your “situation” or “challenge”. Once you have that you can look at the general overview for how the two cards interact with each other.

The benefit of this spread is that it can give clarity when you’re feeling uncertain or anxious. Once you have clarity of thought, and a strong idea of what your challenge is you can find solutions to help your situation

### The 2 card “Yes or No”

Setup: The 2 card “Yes or No” is where you place two cards face up side by side, whilst asking your deck a “yes or no” question.

Interpretation: Each card in a tarot deck has a “yes” or a “no” meaning. Look at the meanings of each card, and you will have your answer.

The benefit of this is you get a stronger yes or no compared to a single card draw. You may find you often get a mix of “yes or no”, and this is fine. It simply means there is doubt, or things are not yet clear.

## What does King Of Swords mean?

The King of Swords tarot card stands for health in a very literal sense. It is a card of strength and determination, symbolizing the ability to be focused, disciplined and in control of one’s physical and mental health. The card speaks to taking the right steps to maintain good physical and mental health, such as eating right and exercising, as well as avoiding unhealthy habits like drinking and smoking. Having a clear and strategic plan of action when it comes to one’s health—such as setting a goal and tracking progress—will lead to better overall outcomes.

Finances

The King of Swords tarot card has implications for finances as well. It symbolizes a leader who is in control of their own finances, who takes a strategic and thoughtful approach when it comes to money. This can mean budgeting and tracking spending, creating and following a financial plan, and understanding investments and other financial instruments. It also means being intentional and thoughtful when it comes to big financial decisions, like getting loans or buying property. The King of Swords also speaks to taking on financial responsibility rather than relying too heavily on others.

Relationships

The King of Swords tarot card stands for relationships as well. It represents a leader who is in control of their emotions and actions when it comes to relationships. This means being honest, open, and clear-headed when communicating with a partner, not letting emotions run away and cloud judgement. It also speaks to being strategic in forging and maintaining relationships, such as looking for compatible partners and taking the time to nurture bonds with friends and family members. Finally, the King of Swords tarot card stands for taking responsibility for one’s own actions and taking the time to reflect and grow in relationships.
