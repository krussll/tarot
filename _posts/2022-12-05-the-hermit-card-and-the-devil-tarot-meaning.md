A key aspect of reading your own tarot is interpreting cards. For a beginner interpreting a single card can be fine, as there are plenty of resources to help you out, however interpreting two or more can be very tricky.

However, if you have been practising single card readings for a while a 2 card spread will be a great introduction into reading multiple cards.

As you’ve found this page, you’re probably wondering how to interpret the The Hermit card and The Devil card together in particular.

The The Hermit card and The Devil have unique qualities and will interact with each other in different ways depending on what kind of reading you are doing.

## What does The Hermit and The Devil mean together?

The Hermit tarot card and The Devil tarot card together for me is about recognizing and understanding our materialistic tendencies and being in tune with our inner selves to gain clarity and avoid the dangers that come with indulging in lust, greed, and addiction. It’s an invitation to retreat into the depths of our spirits to access our inner truth and personal growth, and from there, to be more aware of our physical desires, and to not let them carry us away. By tuning into our higher selves and developing greater understanding of ourselves, we can come away with self-illumination, and this understanding can help us avoid slipping into temptations and the vices of The Devil card.

The meaning may differ depending on what you are asking. Here are some common questions and their possible meanings

It’s time to take a hard look at the patterns and patterns of behavior that are not serving you, and challenge them. The Devil card holds the message that you may be feeling tempted by unhealthy desires such as greed, envy, and materialism. You may be tempted to focus too much on material rewards and comforts, or to make decisions based on short-term gratification, instead of what’s in the best interest of the relationship. You may be also feeling temptations related to sexuality and lust, or the attachment to unhealthy ways of dealing with emotions.

The Hermit and Devil suggest the importance of taking a step back and reflecting on the root of these desires, and how they may be running your relationship. It’s time to identify the true sources of these desires, and to look at how they are impacting the dynamic of your relationship. It’s time to decide if these desires align with the greater vision of your relationship, and if not, to shift them in a more positive direction. It’s time to focus on lasting, meaningful connection instead of short-term gratification, and to prioritize your values over fleeting desires.

## Common 2-card spreads

Whilst it’s great to know who these two cards interact with each other, it’s important to note that the meaning will depend on the context of your spread

The most common 2 card spreads I recommend to newer readers are:

- The 2 card cross (otherwise known as “The Fool’s Journey”)
- The 2 card “Yes or No”

### “The Fool’s Journey”

Setup: The two card cross is where you place two cards face up, one placed vertically and with one laying horizontally over

Interpretation: The 2 cards represent a “situation” and a “challenge”. The first card will describe the situation you are in with the second horizontal card being the “challenge” crossing you (literally crossing you in this case!)

Reading: Using the overviews in this guide is a great way to start. First, look at the meanings of the 2 cards, and see how they might relate either as your “situation” or “challenge”. Once you have that you can look at the general overview for how the two cards interact with each other.

The benefit of this spread is that it can give clarity when you’re feeling uncertain or anxious. Once you have clarity of thought, and a strong idea of what your challenge is you can find solutions to help your situation

### The 2 card “Yes or No”

Setup: The 2 card “Yes or No” is where you place two cards face up side by side, whilst asking your deck a “yes or no” question.

Interpretation: Each card in a tarot deck has a “yes” or a “no” meaning. Look at the meanings of each card, and you will have your answer.

The benefit of this is you get a stronger yes or no compared to a single card draw. You may find you often get a mix of “yes or no”, and this is fine. It simply means there is doubt, or things are not yet clear.

## Yes or No meaning

**Both The Hermit and The Devil mean “No”. If you are asking a direct question, then be under no illusions, the answer is a negative one. If you draw The Hermit and The Devil the answer to your question is “NO”.**

The “Yes” and “No” meanings can differ from reader to reader. The meanings here are based on what I believe are the generally accepted definitions.

## What does The Hermit mean?

Health:

The Hermit Tarot card speaks to the importance of becoming still, of listening deeply to one’s own inner voice and connecting to a higher power. To have good health one must be willing to take time to be alone, to focus inward and to pay attention to spiritual and physical needs. The Hermit Tarot card encourages us to practice mindfulness, to be aware of our habits, our bodies, and our thoughts. When we learn to pay attention to our own physical, mental and emotional health, we are better able to take care of our bodies and minds.

Finances:

The Hermit Tarot card can also bring guidance regarding finances. The card speaks to the need for introspection and wisdom when making financial decisions. It encourages us to deeply consider our goals and resources before taking action. We must be willing to invest time and energy in learning about our finances, evaluating our current situation and strategizing how we can make wise decisions that align with our values and objectives. The Hermit Tarot card reminds us to take the time to understand our finances, to seek advice from experts and to trust our own instinct when it comes to making financial decisions.

Relationships:

The Hermit Tarot card may also provide insight into our relationships. The card can remind us that in order to foster healthy connections, we must be willing to go within and reflect on our relationships. We must become aware of our own needs, doubts and fears. We must be willing to assess our patterns, our modes of communication, and the impact we have on our relationships. The Hermit Tarot card can help us to gain clarity on our relationships and to find the courage and strength to pursue true connection with those closest to us.

## What does The Devil mean?

The Devil Tarot card is associated with the negative aspects of human nature: addiction, greed, envy, materialism, obsession and sexual lust. The energy of this card suggests a lack of will-power, self-destructive habits or destructive behavior.

When interpreted in relation to health, The Devil Tarot card can indicate an unhealthy lifestyle, substance abuse, mental health issues, and an overall lack of self-care. It suggests that a person is putting their physical health at risk by not taking the necessary steps to stay healthy. The Devil Tarot card can also symbolize compulsive behavior, risky choices and poor decision-making that could have long-term negative implications if not addressed.

When interpreted in relation to finances, The Devil Tarot card can symbolize poor money-management skills, excessive spending, and the inability to save or plan for the future. The Devil Tarot card suggests an addiction to material possessions and an extreme desire to acquire wealth at any cost, which can lead to financial instability and debt.

When interpreted in relation to relationships, The Devil Tarot card can point to abusive behavior, codependency, emotional manipulation and destruction of trust. This card can also represent a tendency to prioritize materialistic pursuits over emotional connections and human relationships, which can lead to long-term damage and conflict.

The Devil Tarot card is therefore an important reminder to be mindful of our thoughts, words and actions. It is a reminder to take responsibility for our decisions and to maintain a balance between healthy habits and unhealthy ones. Ultimately, The Devil Tarot card is a call to work towards personal growth, self-improvement and self-love.
