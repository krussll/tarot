A key aspect of reading your own tarot is interpreting cards. For a beginner interpreting a single card can be fine, as there are plenty of resources to help you out, however interpreting two or more can be very tricky.

However, if you have been practising single card readings for a while a 2 card spread will be a great introduction into reading multiple cards.

As you’ve found this page, you’re probably wondering how to interpret the The Devil card and 7 Of Wands card together in particular.

The The Devil card and 7 Of Wands have unique qualities and will interact with each other in different ways depending on what kind of reading you are doing.

## What does The Devil and 7 Of Wands mean together?

The Devil tarot card combined with the 7 of Wands suggests that you may be struggling with an unhealthy obsession or addiction to something, such as material possessions, the opposite sex, or some other craving. You may find yourself trapped in a cycle of sacrificing your health and wellbeing to pursue a certain goal. With the 7 of Wands supplementing the Devil card, you are being encouraged to confront such motivations and to protect yourself from competition and temptation. Your true strength lies within your courage to take action and break free from the attachments or obsessions that have been weighing you down. It is time to fight for your independence and refrain from becoming a slave to your desires.

## Common 2-card spreads

Whilst it’s great to know who these two cards interact with each other, it’s important to note that the meaning will depend on the context of your spread

The most common 2 card spreads I recommend to newer readers are:

- The 2 card cross (otherwise known as “The Fool’s Journey”)
- The 2 card “Yes or No”

### “The Fool’s Journey”

Setup: The two card cross is where you place two cards face up, one placed vertically and with one laying horizontally over

Interpretation: The 2 cards represent a “situation” and a “challenge”. The first card will describe the situation you are in with the second horizontal card being the “challenge” crossing you (literally crossing you in this case!)

Reading: Using the overviews in this guide is a great way to start. First, look at the meanings of the 2 cards, and see how they might relate either as your “situation” or “challenge”. Once you have that you can look at the general overview for how the two cards interact with each other.

The benefit of this spread is that it can give clarity when you’re feeling uncertain or anxious. Once you have clarity of thought, and a strong idea of what your challenge is you can find solutions to help your situation

### The 2 card “Yes or No”

Setup: The 2 card “Yes or No” is where you place two cards face up side by side, whilst asking your deck a “yes or no” question.

Interpretation: Each card in a tarot deck has a “yes” or a “no” meaning. Look at the meanings of each card, and you will have your answer.

The benefit of this is you get a stronger yes or no compared to a single card draw. You may find you often get a mix of “yes or no”, and this is fine. It simply means there is doubt, or things are not yet clear.

## What does The Devil mean?

The Devil Tarot card is an important reminder of the negative consequences that can occur when someone allows themselves to become obsessed with something. When it comes to health, The Devil Tarot card can represent the potential problems that arise when someone mistakes mere physical pleasure for true health. For instance, indulging in too much of a certain type of food or alcohol can temporarily make someone feel satisfied and good, but it can ultimately lead to health problems like obesity, liver disease, or diabetes. An obsession with a certain physical activity can also lead to injury or even death if not done in moderation.

When it comes to finances, The Devil Tarot card can represent a person’s desire to accumulate more and more money and material possessions. This craving for money and possessions can lead someone to make questionable financial decisions, such as taking out risky loans, making foolish investments, or trying to get rich quick by gambling. This can be especially dangerous during times of economic crisis, when it is more important than ever to be financially responsible.

The Devil Tarot card is also a reminder of the dangers of materialism in relationships. Allowing material things to become more important than people can lead to a lack of trust and communication. It can also lead to envy and jealousy, as people often start to compare themselves to others based on possessions. This can be destructive to relationships, as it can lead to power struggles or feelings of resentments.

The Devil Tarot card is an important reminder of the dangers of addiction, greed, envy, and materialism. It can lead to health problems, financial disaster, and issues in relationships. If a person allows themselves to become obsessed with something, it is important to remember to stay disciplined and to keep things in perspective.

## What does 7 Of Wands mean?

The 7 of Wands Tarot card is a card of self-defense, indicating the need to protect one’s own interests in competitive situations. On a health level, this card speaks to the need to fortify one’s overall wellbeing by being proactive, rather than reactive. This might mean investing in preventative health care, pursuing wellness practices such as mindfulness, exercise, and nutrition, and taking the time to nurture and look after oneself.

When it comes to finance and wealth, the 7 of Wands Tarot card encourages maintaining an organized approach to financial management and taking action to safeguard one’s assets. This could mean researching and investing responsibly, steering clear of high-risk ventures, and engaging with financial professionals to ensure one’s financial future is secure.

In terms of relationships, the 7 of Wands Tarot card speaks to the need for self-protection and assertiveness. This might mean setting boundaries around how and when one wishes to be treated, establishing and communicating one’s needs to partners, and being mindful of when one might be giving more than one is comfortable with. It can also be helpful to assess relationships periodically, to ensure that they are still healthy and balanced.
