A key aspect of reading your own tarot is interpreting cards. For a beginner interpreting a single card can be fine, as there are plenty of resources to help you out, however interpreting two or more can be very tricky.

However, if you have been practising single card readings for a while a 2 card spread will be a great introduction into reading multiple cards.

As you’ve found this page, you’re probably wondering how to interpret the The Hanged Man card and 2 Of Swords card together in particular.

The The Hanged Man card and 2 Of Swords have unique qualities and will interact with each other in different ways depending on what kind of reading you are doing.

## What does The Hanged Man and 2 Of Swords mean together?

The Hanged Man tarot card and The 2 Of Swords tarot card, when looked at together, signify the need for a wise and self-sacrificing decision. They suggest that you must be willing to change your viewpoint and find a new perspective on the situation in order to make the best decision. You may need to be prepared to accept a certain degree of compromise in order to reach a mutually beneficial outcome, but if you take the time to look at things from different angles and make the attempt to understand another person’s point of view, you can use this opportunity to grow and learn.

The meaning may differ depending on what you are asking. Here are some common questions and their possible meanings

The Hanged Man advises that wisdom and self-sacrifice can be necessary for obtaining a different point of view, which is often necessary when it comes to making an important decision in a relationship.

The 2 Of Swords is an indication that it’s time to make a compromise or tough decision. It calls for a balance between two different perspectives, not just one. This can be a difficult but necessary process in order to properly assess the situation in order to make the best decision. In this case, The Hanged Man tarot card is a reminder that the decision-making process requires thoughtfulness and consideration in order to be successful.

Ultimately, the combination of The Hanged Man and 2 Of Swords tarot cards suggests that compromise and mediation are the best strategies to take in order to make a positive decision in your relationship. As the saying goes “the two shall become one”, the two perspectives should come together to form a new outlook that is beneficial for both parties involved. With a little bit of insight, you can use the combination of these two tarot cards to bridge any differences between the two of you. Take the time to understand the perspectives of your partner and find the middle ground that both of you can be happy with.

## Common 2-card spreads

Whilst it’s great to know who these two cards interact with each other, it’s important to note that the meaning will depend on the context of your spread

The most common 2 card spreads I recommend to newer readers are:

- The 2 card cross (otherwise known as “The Fool’s Journey”)
- The 2 card “Yes or No”

### “The Fool’s Journey”

Setup: The two card cross is where you place two cards face up, one placed vertically and with one laying horizontally over

Interpretation: The 2 cards represent a “situation” and a “challenge”. The first card will describe the situation you are in with the second horizontal card being the “challenge” crossing you (literally crossing you in this case!)

Reading: Using the overviews in this guide is a great way to start. First, look at the meanings of the 2 cards, and see how they might relate either as your “situation” or “challenge”. Once you have that you can look at the general overview for how the two cards interact with each other.

The benefit of this spread is that it can give clarity when you’re feeling uncertain or anxious. Once you have clarity of thought, and a strong idea of what your challenge is you can find solutions to help your situation

### The 2 card “Yes or No”

Setup: The 2 card “Yes or No” is where you place two cards face up side by side, whilst asking your deck a “yes or no” question.

Interpretation: Each card in a tarot deck has a “yes” or a “no” meaning. Look at the meanings of each card, and you will have your answer.

The benefit of this is you get a stronger yes or no compared to a single card draw. You may find you often get a mix of “yes or no”, and this is fine. It simply means there is doubt, or things are not yet clear.

The “Yes” and “No” meanings can differ from reader to reader. The meanings here are based on what I believe are the generally accepted definitions.

## What does 2 Of Swords mean?

The Two of Swords Tarot Card is a powerful card that can represent a time in which someone’s health, finances, and relationships are on the brink. It can represent a time of tension and struggle that may ultimately lead to one of two paths: compromise or decision. Ultimately, the card is a call to consider both sides of a situation and come to the best possible resolution.

For health, the Two of Swords Tarot Card is a call to look at the efficacy of your current health plan and make changes if necessary. Consider the option of compromise, such as alternative treatments, lifestyle changes, or diet plans that can help improve wellness. The card is also a reminder to seek help from medical professionals if needed.

For finances, the Two of Swords Tarot Card is a reminder to consider all equitable options when making financial decisions. It urges caution and thoughtfulness when it comes to investment and spending. It also encourages setting up a budget in order to make sure all bills are paid on time and resources are used as efficiently as possible.

For relationships, the Two of Swords Tarot Card is a reminder to remain open-minded. It urges compromise and listening to the other person’s point of view. It can also refer to making decisions that will benefit the relationship in the long run, such as joining forces on a project or taking steps to reconnect.

In summary, the Two of Swords Tarot Card is a reminder to look at all options when it comes to our health, finances, and relationships. The card is a call to compromise and be open to different working solutions, with an emphasis on creating an equitable outcome for all parties.
